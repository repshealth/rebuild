Inside the app

Open it and you
already know what to do.

No spreadsheets, no PDF you download once and never open again, no fifteen apps arguing with each other. Here is what you actually get.

01

Today's session, already written

Open the app and the session is there. Every exercise, every set, with a video if you have not done it before. You tick them off as you go and the app remembers where you got to.

02

Your whole week, planned

Three, four or five days depending on what you can actually commit to. Miss one and nothing breaks, it just moves. No guilt, no starting the week again.

03

Food without the spreadsheet

Scan a barcode, log a meal, done. Your calories and protein are worked out from your details rather than pulled off a chart, and you can swap anything you will not eat.

04

Meals you would actually cook

A full plan for the day with four options for every meal. Swap the ones you do not fancy. Nothing on here needs a blender or an afternoon free.

05

Three things a day

Not twenty. Steps, water, and getting off your phone before bed. The app syncs with your watch so most of it tracks itself, and the streak is the bit that keeps you honest.

06

Proof it is working

Weight, photos, measurements and lifts all in one place, so you can see it moving in the weeks when it does not feel like it is.

Meet James

I built this
because I needed it.

I am James Haskell. I started in health and fitness at 15 and spent nearly twenty years as a professional rugby player, 77 caps for England and a British and Irish Lion. I played around the world and worked with some of the best coaches, trainers and nutritionists in the game.

Then I turned 40 and realised I was not where I wanted to be. A divorce, losing my dad, multiple surgeries, constant travel, businesses to run and a kid to be a dad to. Somewhere in all that I stopped doing the things I had spent thirty years learning how to do.

So I built it around one question: how do you rebuild yourself when real life does not stop? I could not disappear to a training camp for twelve weeks. It had to work around the life I actually had, not some imaginary perfect routine. That is what is in here, and I am in here with you.
